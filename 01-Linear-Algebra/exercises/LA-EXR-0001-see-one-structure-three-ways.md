# See One Structure Three Ways

## Task

Choose two vectors with two or three components. Give them a meaning from a real or invented context: movement, inventory, sensor readings, color, budget categories, or another interpretation.

Produce four things:

1. write the vectors as coordinates;
2. explain what vector addition means in your context;
3. explain what multiplying one vector by a scalar means;
4. reinterpret the same coordinates geometrically, then state what information the geometric picture preserves and what contextual meaning it forgets.

Finally, define one rule that maps your vectors to new vectors. Decide whether you think the rule is linear and justify your answer using at least one of:

$$
T(u+v)=T(u)+T(v), \qquad T(cu)=cT(u), \qquad T(0)=0.
$$

## Evidence of success

A successful response does not merely perform arithmetic. It explicitly separates:

- the **coordinates**;
- the **meaning assigned by the model**;
- the **operations**;
- the **linearity test**.

Someone reading your explanation should be able to see why the same algebra can support several interpretations.

## Optional challenge

Construct a rule that passes the zero-vector test but is still not linear. This shows why `T(0)=0` is necessary but not sufficient.
