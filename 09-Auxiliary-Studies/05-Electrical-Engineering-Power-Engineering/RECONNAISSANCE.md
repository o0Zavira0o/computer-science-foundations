# Reconnaissance — Electrical Engineering — Power Engineering

**Track:** `electrical-engineering-power` (`PWR`)
**Baseline audit date:** 2026-08-26

## Entry model

The track is designed for zero subject-specific knowledge. It does not assume a prior circuits or electromagnetics course.

## Baseline sources

The curriculum was cross-checked against:

- MIT OpenCourseWare 6.061 and its open textbook for classical electric-power foundations;
- U.S. Department of Energy material for generation/transmission/distribution and grid infrastructure;
- NIST smart-grid architecture for modern interoperability, DER, communications, and cybersecurity context;
- NERC reliability-standard families for balancing, operations, planning, protection, voltage/reactive control, emergency operation, and critical infrastructure;
- PJM educational material for operator/system context.

## Coverage decisions

The graph deliberately starts below the normal advanced-undergraduate power-systems prerequisite line, then progresses through:

1. electrical quantities and circuits;
2. AC and three-phase foundations;
3. magnetics, transformers, and machines;
4. transmission and distribution;
5. power flow and operations;
6. faults, protection, grounding, stability, voltage, and frequency control;
7. power electronics, HVDC/FACTS, renewables, storage, DERs, and microgrids;
8. monitoring, reliability, markets, optimization, planning, standards, cybersecurity, and research literacy.

## Visual policy

Physical-grid, machine, transformer, substation, protection, and converter lessons should use selective verified visuals when physical form materially improves understanding. Diagrams should not be added merely as decoration.

## Frontier policy

Standards, grid codes, reliability guidance, inverter-based-resource practice, and current deployment claims are version-sensitive. Refresh authoritative sources before making current-status claims.
