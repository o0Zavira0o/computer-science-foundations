# Philosophy and Logic Curriculum Reconnaissance

**Audit date:** 2026-08-19
**Entry model:** absolute zero subject-specific knowledge
**Status:** audited initial dependency spine, not a permanent endpoint

## Purpose

This reconnaissance establishes an evidence-backed starting graph for a combined Philosophy and Logic track. The track is intentionally broader than an introductory “critical thinking” course and broader than a formal-logic sequence alone.

A learner should be able to enter without prior philosophy or symbolic logic, develop disciplined argument and formal reasoning, encounter major philosophical fields and globally significant traditions, and continue into specialized graduate and research work.

## Source classes cross-checked

The baseline deliberately combines different kinds of evidence rather than copying one syllabus:

1. an open, peer-reviewed introductory philosophy textbook with thematic and global coverage (`PHL-REF-001`);

6. explicit historical and global reference points, including ancient logic, classical Indian epistemology, and Islamic metaphysics (`PHL-REF-016`–`PHL-REF-018`);
7. technical bridge material for paradox, incompleteness, and formal epistemology (`PHL-REF-015`, `PHL-REF-019`).

## Scope decisions

The track includes both **doing philosophy** and **studying philosophy**. Historical figures are not presented as a sequence of names to memorize; their texts and positions become occasions for argument reconstruction, conceptual comparison, and historical interpretation.

Formal logic is treated as a genuine technical subject. It begins with ordinary arguments and propositional/predicate logic, then reaches metalogic, computability, modal logic, selected non-classical logics, and philosophy of logic.

The philosophical survey includes epistemology, metaphysics, ethics, political philosophy, aesthetics, religion, mind, language, science, mathematics, law, technology, social ontology, and related fields. It also avoids treating philosophy as an exclusively modern Western lineage: the curriculum makes room for Indian, Chinese, Islamic, African/Africana, feminist, analytic, continental, and other relevant traditions while preserving historical differences instead of forcing false equivalence.

## Level interpretation

- **L0:** learn what philosophical inquiry and arguments are; gain first informal/formal reasoning tools.
- **L1:** foundational formal logic plus core fields and major historical/global orientations.
- **L2:** core undergraduate depth, metalogic foundations, advanced field coverage, and broader traditions.
- **L3:** advanced undergraduate work in non-classical logic, specialized debates, and comparative/historical method.
- **L4:** graduate-style specialization and interdisciplinary research methods.
- **L5:** research literacy: primary-text scholarship, literature search, formal projects, prospectus writing, peer criticism, and citation ethics.
- **L6:** dated, open-ended research frontier and original contribution work.

## Non-goals

Completeness does **not** mean assigning one lesson to every philosopher, school, or named argument in the history of thought. That would create an encyclopedia without a learning architecture.

The goal is an auditable core with expandable branches. New nodes should be added when real conceptual, historical, methodological, or research gaps are discovered.

## Publication rule

Lesson prose is authored one node at a time. Future lessons must preserve distinctions among:

- textual/historical claims;
- empirical claims;
- normative claims;
- conceptual analysis;
- formal logical results;
- the author's or lesson's interpretation.

The track must not smuggle controversial philosophical conclusions into definitions or present one tradition as the neutral default without signaling the choice.
