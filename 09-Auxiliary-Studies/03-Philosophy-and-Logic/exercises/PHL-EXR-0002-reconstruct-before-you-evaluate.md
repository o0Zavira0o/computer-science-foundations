# PHL-EXR-0002 — Reconstruct Before You Evaluate

**Track:** Philosophy and Logic
**Companion lesson:** [`PHL-0002`](../lessons/PHL-0002-questions-claims-reasons-and-arguments.md)

## Goal

Practice separating an open question, candidate conclusion, explicit reasons, and hidden bridge assumptions **before** deciding whether you agree.

## Part A — question or claim?

Classify each item:

```text
1. Is punishment justified by desert?
2. Desert can justify punishment even when deterrence is absent.
3. Explain whether memory is necessary for personal identity.
4. Some true beliefs are not knowledge.
5. Could a machine have moral status?
```

Then rewrite one question as two rival candidate claims.

## Part B — find the conclusion

Read:

> A rule that cannot be publicly explained to the people subject to it is difficult to contest. High-impact institutional decisions should be contestable. Therefore institutions should provide accessible reasons for high-impact decisions.

Write:

```text
Conclusion:
Explicit reason 1:
Explicit reason 2:
Term that needs clarification:
```

Do not evaluate the argument yet.

## Part C — expose a bridge premise

Read:

> People survive major changes in taste. So taste cannot define personal identity.

Write one premise that would connect the observation to the conclusion.

Then ask whether the speaker is claiming that taste is:

- not sufficient for identity;
- not necessary for identity;
- neither;
- something else.

The exercise is designed to reveal how much work compressed language can hide.

## Part D — reason or restatement?

For each pair, decide whether the second sentence supplies independent support or mostly repeats the first.

```text
A. The policy is unjust.
   The policy is unfair.

B. The policy is unjust.
   It imposes a severe burden on one group while giving that group no opportunity to contest the decision.
```

Explain your classification without relying only on the words “same” or “different.”

## Part E — argument or explanation?

Classify the conversational role.

### 1

> We should postpone the hike because the weather service predicts lightning in the area.

### 2

> The power failed because a tree brought down the transmission line; the outage itself is already confirmed.

For each, ask:

> Are the reasons mainly trying to establish that the conclusion should be accepted, or explain why an accepted event occurred?

## Part F — build one argument card

Choose one question:

1. Should anonymous speech be protected in all online communities?
2. Can an action be morally right if done for a bad motive?
3. Does remembering an experience make it *your* experience?
4. Should automated systems be required to give reasons for decisions that significantly affect people?

Complete:

```text
Question:
Candidate claim:
Premise 1:
Premise 2:
Possible hidden bridge premise:
Conclusion:
One term needing clarification:
One premise needing evidence or defense:
```

## Transfer challenge — reconstruct charitably without inventing

Take this compressed statement:

> “Free choice requires alternatives, so a person with literally no possible alternative cannot act freely.”

Produce a numbered reconstruction with no more than three premises.

Then identify one place where you had to interpret the speaker rather than merely copy words.

<details>
<summary>Possible structure</summary>

```text
P1. An action is freely chosen only if the agent has at least one genuine alternative.
P2. A person with literally no possible alternative has no genuine alternative.
C. Therefore, a person with literally no possible alternative does not act freely.
```

The important point is not whether this theory of freedom is correct. The exercise is to make the support structure explicit enough that later lessons can test it.

</details>

## Completion criterion

Given a short paragraph of ordinary prose, you can write:

```text
what is being asked
what is being claimed
what reasons are offered
what conclusion is targeted
what assumption is doing hidden work
```

Only then should you move to full evaluation.
