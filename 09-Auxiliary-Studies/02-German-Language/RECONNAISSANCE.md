# German Language — Curriculum Reconnaissance (2026-08-19)

## Purpose

The German track begins at absolute zero, but it is not designed to stop at an exam certificate. CEFR gives the progression framework through A1–C2; Goethe materials provide externally observable receptive/productive tasks; IDS/grammis and corpus resources prevent advanced grammar from collapsing into oversimplified learner rules; official orthography anchors writing conventions; and German-studies curricula inform the post-C2 language/literature/research branch.

## Progression model

- **L0:** pre-A1/A1 foundations: sound, spelling, first interaction, basic grammar and survival performance.
- **L1:** A2: broader case/tense/subordination systems and connected everyday communication.
- **L2:** B1: independent narration, argument, authentic receptive work, register and integrated grammar.
- **L3:** B2: syntactic density, reported speech, modal particles, academic/professional communication, variation, and literary entry.
- **L4:** C1/C2 and beyond: precision, style, rhetoric, academic/professional German, literature, mediation, language history, corpus-informed judgment, and official orthography.
- **L5:** research literacy in German linguistics, stylistics, discourse, literature, and scholarship.
- **L6:** open corpus/literature/variation research and contribution-grade work.

## Method decisions

Pronunciation/listening start on lesson one; grammar is extracted from meaningful language rather than taught as disconnected tables; vocabulary is learned as phraseology/collocation as proficiency rises; receptive and productive skills are interleaved; and post-C2 work explicitly includes literature and German-language scholarship instead of pretending C2 is a permanent endpoint.

The 102 nodes are an audited spine, not a fixed course length.
